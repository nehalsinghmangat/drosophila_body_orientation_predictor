Reviewer #1:
This manuscript presents an artificial neural network for estimating Drosophila yaw orientation from translational trajectory variables. The practical motivation is clear: many insect-flight datasets contain center-of-mass trajectories but lack direct body-orientation measurements. The manuscript is generally well written, the proposed software tool may be useful, and the authors appropriately discuss several limitations of applying the model outside the experimental conditions represented in the training data.

However, the current evaluation contains important risks of target circularity and train-test leakage. These issues affect the central quantitative result—the reported angular error—and must be resolved before the conclusions can be supported.

Major comments

1. The target heading is partly constructed from a feature later used to predict it.

The raw camera-derived body orientation is ambiguous modulo π. To resolve this ambiguity, the authors use inferred thrust direction in both the initial heuristic and the mixed-integer optimization. The neural network is then trained using thrust magnitude and thrust angle among its input variables.

This creates a potentially circular evaluation. The corrected target is not independent of the predictor variables: the correction procedure explicitly selects the orientation that aligns with thrust, especially when thrust is large. A model receiving thrust angle as input may therefore learn, at least partly, the rule used to construct its labels rather than recover independently measured body orientation.

This concern is particularly important because the optimization objective directly penalizes disagreement between corrected heading and thrust direction. The authors' interpretation that the network infers body orientation from translational dynamics is consequently difficult to separate from the possibility that it reproduces the thrust-based correction prior.

The authors should quantify this circularity through additional experiments:

Evaluate against a subset of frames or trajectories for which the anterior-posterior body direction can be determined independently from image information, manual annotation, asymmetric body features, or another measurement not based on thrust.
Train and test models without thrust-derived inputs.
Compare the ANN against the heading produced directly by the correction prior, including thrust direction alone and the complete heuristic or optimization-based estimator.
Report performance separately for frames in which the raw ellipse orientation is unambiguous or can be independently disambiguated.
Perform sensitivity analyses over the ratio α

Until an independent validation is provided, the output should be described as an estimate consistent with the authors' biomechanical prior, not as validated recovery of true body orientation.

2. The train-test splitting procedure appears vulnerable to severe leakage.

The manuscript states that delay-embedded feature-target pairs were randomly divided using a 67:33 split. It does not state that complete trajectories, individual flies, recording sessions, or experiments were assigned exclusively to training or test sets.

Randomly splitting frames is inappropriate for temporally correlated trajectory data. Consecutive examples use overlapping windows of four time points, so neighboring samples may share most of their input values. If adjacent windows from the same trajectory occur in both sets, the test samples are not statistically independent of the training data. The model may also exploit trajectory-specific wind conditions, spatial regions, or behavioral patterns.

All preprocessing, model selection, cross-validation, and final testing should be repeated with grouped splits at the complete-trajectory level. If animal identities or recording sessions are available, a stronger evaluation should hold out animals or sessions. Grouped cross-validation such as GroupKFold should be used during hyperparameter selection, with the group identifier never divided across folds.

The manuscript should explicitly report:

The number of trajectories in each training, validation, and test partition.
Whether multiple trajectories came from the same fly.
The number of flies, recording sessions, and experimental trials.
The grouping variable used for splitting.
Whether all frames from a trajectory remain in one partition.

[STATUS — Addressed.
  - Notebook: the outer train/test split now uses `GroupShuffleSplit` keyed on `trajec_objid` (67%/33%), guaranteeing no trajectory -- nor any of its rotation-augmented copies -- contributes frames to both sides. This is used identically for the OLS baselines and the ANN.
  - Notebook: the inner train/validation split used during `model.fit` also uses a grouped split (`grouped_inner_val_split_with_weights`, keyed on `trajec_objid`, seed 123) rather than Keras's random `validation_split`.
  - Notebook/manuscript: the new architecture/loss hyperparameter search (added this round) uses grouped 3-fold cross-validation (`GroupKFold`, keyed on `trajec_objid`), restricted to the training trajectories only, so model selection never touches the held-out test set.
  - Manuscript (Methods) explicitly reports the number of trajectories per partition (e.g. train: 2,235; test: 1,099-1,101 depending on filter), states the grouping variable (`trajec_objid`), and states that no trajectory contributes to both sets. NOT reported: fly/animal identity, recording-session or trial counts, or whether multiple trajectories came from the same fly -- this information is not present as a field in the underlying dataset as used here, so it could not be added without a different upstream data source.]

3. Rotation augmentation may introduce additional duplication across evaluation sets.

The manuscript constructs an unaugmented dataset and a randomly rotated copy and then states that the same random split was applied to both versions. The precise sequence of splitting, augmentation, and concatenation is unclear.

Augmentation must occur only after trajectories have been assigned to training, validation, and test partitions. Rotated versions of a training trajectory must never appear in validation or test data, and the unrotated and rotated forms of a test trajectory should not be treated as independent test observations.

Please provide pseudocode or a flow diagram showing exactly when trajectory splitting, delay embedding, augmentation, concatenation, cross-validation, and final evaluation occur. The safest procedure is:

Divide complete original trajectories into grouped train, validation, and test sets.
Fit all preprocessing choices using training trajectories only.
Apply augmentation only to the training trajectories.
Evaluate once on untouched, nonaugmented held-out trajectories.

[STATUS — Addressed.
  - Notebook/manuscript: the pipeline now follows exactly the "safest procedure" listed here. The trajectory-grouped split happens first; rotation augmentation is then applied only to the training trajectories, producing a duplicated (original + rotated) training set, while `test_embedded` is built from the held-out test trajectories with `wind_augment=False` and is never duplicated or rotated. This is stated explicitly in the manuscript ("Rotational augmentation was applied only to the training trajectories after this split; the withheld test set contained only unaugmented trajectories," Methods, Neural Network Training) and is directly visible in the notebook's "Training the Model" data-prep cell, which builds `train_all` (original + rotated) and `test_embedded` (unaugmented only) from already-separated `train_trajectories`/`test_trajectories`.
  - No pseudocode/flow diagram figure was added; the notebook itself (which runs top-to-bottom in this order) serves as an executable version of that procedure.]

The primary unit of replication should be the trajectory, animal, or recording session. Please report:

The distribution of median or mean angular error across held-out trajectories.
Median, interquartile range, and bootstrap confidence intervals obtained by resampling complete trajectories or animals.
The number and proportion of trajectories exceeding practically meaningful error thresholds.
Results from several independently initialized training runs.

A trajectory-level cumulative distribution or box/violin plot would be more informative than only a frame-density plot.

Reviewer #2:


Observations

1. The train/test split appears vulnerable to severe temporal leakage

This is my most important concern.

The manuscript says:

"The dataset of feature–target pairs was split into training and withheld test sets (67-33 split)."

The delay embedding uses a four-frame backward window, so consecutive training examples necessarily share most of their input observations.

If the split was performed randomly over individual feature-target pairs, frames from the same trajectory will occur in both training and test sets. Because the data are sampled at 0.01 s and the input windows overlap heavily, the network may effectively see almost the same trajectory segment during training and testing.

This is particularly problematic because the paper describes performance as being evaluated on a "withheld test set," which may give the impression of an independent test set.

I strongly recommend that the authors clarify exactly what was randomized. If the split was frame-level, the analysis should be repeated with splitting performed at the trajectory/fly level.

The manuscript needs stronger baseline models

The paper demonstrates that the ANN works, but it does not adequately establish that an ANN is necessary.

The authors already show that ground velocity is an imperfect proxy and that air velocity is more closely associated with heading.

However, the relevant question is:

Does the ANN provide substantially better prediction than simpler models?

I would expect at least the following baselines:

ground-velocity direction;
air-velocity direction;
thrust direction;
perhaps a weighted combination of air velocity and thrust;
a simple linear/circular regression model;
ANN without time-delay embedding;
ANN without rotational augmentation;
ANN without thrust features.

The current comparison mainly demonstrates ANN predictions versus ground velocity and reports the ANN's angular error. That is insufficient to establish the added value of the proposed architecture.

[STATUS — Addressed.

The authors should specify exactly how GridSearchCV was configured and whether groups were supplied.
